# Fuzzy-ML drug-release study — results summary

Generated by `python run_study.py`. All artifacts are in `study_results/`.

> **Read this first.** The study's contribution is **interpretability and
> boundary robustness of the predicted release**, not predictive accuracy. Tasks
> A–F below answer the accuracy question (largely: fuzzy does not help). Tasks
> G–I answer the questions the project is actually about. See `CHANGES.md` for
> what was corrected and why, and `proposal.md` for the framing.
>
> Results are organised against the five evaluation questions:
> **A** predictive performance (Task D) · **B** boundary robustness (Task G) ·
> **C** interpretability (Task G) · **D** rule usefulness (Task H) ·
> **E** temporal consistency (Task H).

## Setup

| | |
|---|---|
| Rows | 4,913 measurements → 4,909 after same-time averaging |
| Formulations | 321 |
| Drug groups (exact SMILES) | 88 |
| Split | `GroupKFold(5)` on exact `Drug SMILES` |
| Drugs appearing in >1 fold | **0** (verified) |
| Missing values | 0 — no imputation |
| Release clipping / monotonicity forcing | none |
| Predictor | XGBoost, one fixed untuned config, seed 42, identical in every arm |

Fuzzy knots, the crisp scaler and the classification undersampler are fitted on
the training fold only. For the measurement-level task they are fitted on the
training fold's **formulation** rows, not its measurement rows, so a 48-point
curve does not pull the quantiles ten times harder than a 5-point curve.

Drug name, SMILES and DOI are used for grouping and provenance only, never as
model inputs.

## Task C — what fuzzification actually did

Triangular Low/Medium/High with shoulders at q25/q50/q75, refit inside each fold.

**Tied-knot fallback (declared).** If the raw q25/q50/q75 are not strictly
increasing, the same quantiles are recomputed over the feature's *distinct*
observed values. This fired on exactly two features, both predicted by the audit:
`LA/GA` (71% one value) and `Solubility Enhancer Concentration` (64% exactly
zero). Equal-width was not used as the fallback because it is run as a separate
arm; epsilon-widening was rejected because on a feature that is 64% zero it
leaves a near-binary indicator whose scale is arbitrary. No feature was
unfuzzifiable in any fold.

**Saturation** — share of held-out rows with High membership exactly 1:

| feature | quantile knots | equal-width knots |
|---|---|---|
| Initial Drug-to-Polymer Ratio | 0.430 | 0.003 |
| Polymer MW | 0.318 | 0.009 |
| LA/GA | 0.277 | 0.012 |
| Drug TPSA | 0.265 | 0.022 |
| Particle Size | 0.256 | 0.003 |
| Drug MW | 0.247 | 0.003 |

Quantile shoulders flatten the top quartile of every long-tailed feature into one
label — Polymer MW values from 46 to 215 become indistinguishable. Equal-width
knots remove that, but only by pushing nearly all mass into `Low`, which is a
different distortion rather than a fix. Both are run as arms in Task D.

### Classification (AUC ≤ 0.5 vs > 0.5)

74.5% of formulations are AUC > 0.5 (~2.9:1). Training folds are undersampled;
validation folds keep their natural prevalence, so accuracy must be read against
that rate, not against 50%.

| representation | accuracy | precision | recall | F1 | AUROC |
|:---|:---|:---|:---|:---|:---|
| crisp | 0.6412 ± 0.0970 | 0.8028 ± 0.0705 | 0.6741 ± 0.1586 | 0.7245 ± 0.1000 | 0.5842 ± 0.1724 |
| fuzzy (quantile) | 0.6102 ± 0.0820 | 0.7759 ± 0.0859 | 0.6526 ± 0.1666 | 0.6996 ± 0.1119 | 0.5523 ± 0.1643 |
| fuzzy (uniform) | 0.6163 ± 0.1179 | 0.7979 ± 0.0782 | 0.6450 ± 0.1699 | 0.7019 ± 0.1126 | 0.5868 ± 0.1712 |
| hybrid (quantile) | 0.6382 ± 0.0858 | 0.8124 ± 0.0809 | 0.6579 ± 0.1673 | 0.7155 ± 0.1089 | 0.5851 ± 0.1323 |
| hybrid (uniform) | 0.6100 ± 0.1090 | 0.7964 ± 0.0846 | 0.6287 ± 0.1662 | 0.6921 ± 0.1158 | 0.5735 ± 0.1582 |

Every arm sits near AUROC 0.55–0.59 with fold sd around 0.16 — one fold scores
below 0.35, i.e. worse than chance. **No arm separates the classes reliably, and
fuzzy does not help here.** These are mathematical AUC classes, not clinical
burst-release labels.

Note the AUC class is computed on the *normalized* time axis, so a 3-day and a
238-day profile are summarized the same way. That is the benchmark's definition,
but it discards duration entirely and may be part of why the task is this hard.

## Task E — rules

Extracted from the best predictor (fuzzy uniform, time-dependent). RuleFit and a
depth-3 surrogate tree, both fitted on the training fold against the XGBoost
model's own predictions.

Surrogate fidelity on held-out rows — agreement with the *model*, not with
measured release:

| method | fidelity R² | fidelity RMSE | rules kept |
|---|---|---|---|
| RuleFit | 0.811 | 0.123 | 38.8 |
| Surrogate tree (depth 3) | 0.718 | 0.149 | 8.0 |

234 rules extracted; 181 passed the three held-out tests (coverage ≥ 5%,
direction preserved, drug-clustered bootstrap CI excluding 0).

**That number is misleading on its own, and the reason matters.** Nearly every
extracted rule pairs a formulation condition with a time condition, and release
rises steeply with time — so a rule contrasted against all other rows gets
credited for an effect that time alone explains. Re-testing each formulation
condition *inside the rule's own time window* leaves **14 of 60** formulation
rules standing.

Of those 14, none replicates: the best any condition pattern achieves is **2 of 5
folds**. Under the pre-set threshold of 3 of 5 folds with consistent direction,

> **No rule qualifies as a validated finding. Nothing here should be reported to
> a pharmaceutical scientist as a result.**

The two closest candidates — leads to test on more data, *not* findings:

- `IF Drug MW is not clearly Low AND Log1p Time > 2.46 THEN release is higher by 0.321` (2/5 folds, mean coverage 39%)
- `IF Drug Encapsulation Efficiency is mostly High AND Log1p Time ≤ 2.08 THEN release is lower by 0.456` (2/5 folds, mean coverage 16%)

A further interpretability problem: the best-scoring arm uses equal-width knots,
under which almost all mass falls in `Low`, so conditions like "Polymer MW is not
clearly Medium" are statistically real but linguistically close to vacuous. The
arm that produces meaningful linguistic labels (quantile) is not the arm that
predicts best in this setting.

(formulation 1, the 238-day profile) shows the model predicting near-complete
release within ~50 days against a measured lag phase lasting ~150 days — the
model has no representation of lag behaviour.

## Task G — fuzzy interpretation of the predicted release *(the contribution)*

The model predicts a continuous release value; that value is then given a graded
Low/Medium/High description. This layer is independent of the input encoding and
attaches to any regressor without retraining.

### Where the boundaries come from — stated, not assumed

| provenance | Low/Med knot | Med peak | Med/High knot | status |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| design | 0.3333 | 0.5000 | 0.6667 | **Declared by this study.** Even split of the [0,1] axis. Not a pharmaceutical fact. |
| data | 0.3400 | 0.5712 | 0.7460 | Tertiles of the **training fold's** release distribution. Describes this corpus, not therapy. |

Per-fold data-derived knots are stable (knot 0 ranges 0.305–0.357 across the five
folds), so the pooled row is representative rather than an average over
disagreement.

**No expert-supplied definition was available.** This therefore remains a
*parameterised framework*, and boundary selection is flagged as a
**supervisor / pharmaceutical-expert decision**.

### (C) Interpretability

- **36.9%** of the 4,909 held-out predictions receive a graded, multi-set
  description. A crisp scheme would describe 100% of them with exactly one label.
- **557 rows (11.3%)** are genuinely borderline — no set above 0.65 membership.
  These are the formulations a hard label describes worst.

Real examples from held-out drugs (`taskG_predicted_interpretation.csv`):

| formulation | day | predicted | Low | Medium | High | crisp says |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 2 | 2.00 | 56.0% | 0.00 | 0.64 | 0.36 | only "Medium" |
| 5 | 14.06 | 59.3% | 0.00 | 0.44 | **0.56** | only "Medium" |
| 137 | 3.20 | 41.7% | 0.50 | 0.50 | 0.00 | only "Medium" |

Formulation 5 is the case the project exists for: the crisp scheme reports
"Medium" for a prediction whose *highest* membership is High.

### (B) Boundary robustness

Every prediction nudged by ±1 percentage point — the size of the 69%/70% step.

| interpreter | near a boundary | crisp flip rate | mean fuzzy shift | max fuzzy shift |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| declared (1/3, 2/3) | 196 rows (**4.0%**) | 0.0399 | 0.0438 | 0.120 |
| data-derived tertiles | 236 rows (4.8%) | 0.0481 | 0.0459 | 0.114 |

Restricted to the near-boundary rows: the crisp scheme reassigns **100%** of them
to a different category; the fuzzy description moves by **0.057** on the same
0–1 scale.

> **The 100% is true by construction and is not a finding.** "Near a boundary" is
> *defined* as straddling a cut point, and a crisp scheme flips on exactly that
> set. Reporting it as a discovery would be circular.
>
> The two empirical numbers are: **4.0%** of held-out predictions are exposed to
> a flip at all, and the fuzzy description of that same set changes by **0.057
> instead of 1.0**. The first bounds how much this can matter — **the fuzzy layer
> does nothing for the other 96%**.

### Does the conclusion depend on the boundary choice?

| knot set | near boundary | crisp flip | fuzzy shift | graded share |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| declared (1/3, 2/3) | 0.0399 | 0.0399 | 0.0438 | 0.369 |
| data tertiles | 0.0481 | 0.0481 | 0.0459 | 0.466 |
| narrow (0.25, 0.75) | 0.0491 | 0.0491 | 0.0436 | 0.548 |
| wide (0.4, 0.6) | 0.0458 | 0.0458 | 0.0426 | 0.218 |

**Survives every choice:** the fuzzy shift is roughly an order of magnitude below
the crisp flip rate throughout. **Does not survive:** how many formulations are
affected, and the graded share (0.218 → 0.548, a factor of 2.5) — both are
properties of the chosen boundaries, not of the method.

## Task H — rules within release phases

Time is **excluded from the rule learner entirely**. The data is cut into phases
and a depth-3 tree is fitted inside each, on **measured** release, over the fuzzy
membership columns of **all ten** static descriptors. This replaces the earlier
surrogate-of-XGBoost approach in which time competed and was discounted
afterwards.

| phase | bound | measurements | formulations | drugs | mean release | provenance |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Early | 0–3 d | 1,331 (27.1%) | 321 | 88 | 0.212 | **Supported** — published ≤20%-within-3-days criterion |
| Middle | 3–14 d | 1,886 (38.4%) | 320 | 88 | 0.550 | **Data-derived** (66th pct of times) — needs expert selection |
| Late | >14 d | 1,692 (34.5%) | 250 | 72 | 0.746 | Remainder; inherits the same unvalidated boundary |

The trees fit weakly (mean train R² 0.17–0.36, held-out R² −0.15 to −0.05). That
is expected and is not the criterion: a depth-3 tree on ten descriptors is meant
to be *readable*, and its job is to propose candidates that are then tested.

### (D) Rule usefulness — the negative result

| evidence tier | n rules |
|---|---|
| **validated finding** | **0** |
| replicated | 0 |
| candidate | 9 |
| rejected | 105 |

Thresholds declared in advance: replication in ≥3 of 5 drug-grouped folds with
consistent direction, plus held-out effect ≥0.05 fractional release.

All 9 candidates passed on their own fold but replicated in **exactly 1 of 5**.
Rejections were dominated by the bootstrap interval including zero (105/105
failed that test; 52 also failed coverage, 55 also flipped direction).

> **No rule qualifies as a validated finding. Nothing here should be reported to
> a pharmaceutical scientist as a result.**

This is a *cleaner* negative than the previous version's. Time can no longer be
laundering the effect, so the conclusion is specifically that **ten static
descriptors do not resolve a replicable formulation–release rule at the bar set
in advance** — not that no such relationship exists.

The candidate rules had substantial effect sizes on their own fold (|effect|
0.084–0.449, median 0.169). They failed on *replication*, not on magnitude, which
is the signature of fold-specific rather than absent structure.

## Task I — alternative target (≤20% by day 3), separate experiment

Definition from Pharmaceutics 19(5):767 (2026). Adopted **because a source
defines it**, not because it improves metrics. Observable for all 321
formulations; 35.5% are slow-releasing.

| representation | accuracy | F1 | AUROC |
|---|---|---|---|
| crisp | 0.6416 ± 0.0518 | 0.4741 ± 0.2204 | 0.6687 ± 0.1285 |
| **fuzzy (quantile)** | 0.6574 ± 0.0560 | **0.5319 ± 0.1814** | **0.7114 ± 0.0739** |
| fuzzy (uniform) | 0.6355 ± 0.0328 | 0.4810 ± 0.2045 | 0.6465 ± 0.1137 |
| hybrid (quantile) | **0.6668 ± 0.0526** | 0.5255 ± 0.1934 | 0.6811 ± 0.1026 |
| hybrid (uniform) | 0.6261 ± 0.0257 | 0.4724 ± 0.1962 | 0.6472 ± 0.1079 |

This target is **markedly more learnable than the AUC class** (AUROC 0.67–0.71 vs

discards duration by rescaling the time axis, may simply be the harder and less
meaningful target.

## Conclusion — did fuzzy representation help?

The question has to be split, because the answers differ.

**As a feature encoding (question A): mostly no.** In the time-dependent setting —
the only setting where the models work at all (R² ≈ 0.47–0.49) — every fuzzy and
hybrid arm lands within 0.006 RMSE of crisp with win counts of 2/5 to 4/5, which
is noise. Quantile fuzzification did improve the time-independent targets in 5/5
folds at 24 h and 48 h (Δ ≈ 0.016–0.018, ~8%), but **every arm there has negative
mean R²**: fuzzification made a failing model fail less. On the new ≤20%/day-3
target it is the best arm (AUROC 0.711 vs 0.669 crisp), which is the strongest
accuracy signal in the study and still rests on five folds with ±0.07 sd.
Fuzzy-as-feature-representation is not clearly earning its complexity.

**As an output interpretation (questions B and C): yes, within measured
limits.** 36.9% of held-out predictions receive a graded description; the 4.0% of
predictions sitting within one percentage point of a cut point change their
description by 0.057 instead of being reassigned wholesale. That contrast holds
under every boundary definition tried. The honest bound is equally clear: **the
layer does nothing for the other 96%**, and how many formulations fall in the
affected 4% is a property of boundaries nobody has yet validated.

**As a source of rules (question D): no.** Zero rules reached the
validated-finding tier; all nine candidates replicated in exactly one fold. With
time now excluded structurally rather than discounted afterwards, this is a clean
statement that ten static descriptors do not resolve a replicable
formulation–release rule at the declared bar.

**Temporal structure (question E)** is the one suggestive positive: the phases
demonstrably split on different characteristics. It is a pattern in what the
trees used, not a validated finding.

**The binding constraint remains information, not encoding.** Ten static
descriptors carry limited information about a release curve for an unseen drug,
and that — not the choice between crisp and fuzzy inputs — sets the ceiling. What
the fuzzy layer changes is not how much the model knows but how honestly its
output is described, which is worth having and is smaller than the project's
original framing implied.

### Earlier framing, retained for the record

Mostly no, with one qualified exception. In the time-dependent setting — the only
setting where the models actually work (R² ≈ 0.47–0.49) — every fuzzy and hybrid
arm lands within 0.006 RMSE of crisp, with the winning fold count ranging from
2/5 to 4/5. That is noise, and the honest reading is that fuzzification changes
nothing there. In the time-independent setting, quantile-knot fuzzification
improved RMSE in 5 of 5 folds at both 24 h and 48 h (Δ ≈ 0.016–0.018, roughly 8%)
— the one consistent positive signal in the study, and notably it appears only
with quantile knots, not equal-width. But that signal must be read against the
fact that **every arm in that setting, fuzzy included, has a negative mean R²**:
none beats predicting the held-out fold's own mean. Fuzzification made a failing
model fail less. For classification it helped nowhere. For rule extraction it
produced readable linguistic conditions, but no rule survived the combination of
a held-out effect test, a time-window control, and a cross-fold replication
requirement. The defensible conclusion is that fuzzy-as-feature-representation is
not currently earning its complexity, and that the binding constraint is not the
encoding but the amount of information 10 static descriptors carry about a
release curve for a drug the model has never seen.
